# Gimbal production assets

These assets were originally authored for Orrery, now Zenith. Existing
asset provenance and generator identifiers remain unchanged by the rebrand.

Gimbal is Navigator's original full-character 3D form. The geometry, rig, gesture clips, atlas, icon, and model-sheet renders are generated by `scripts/gimbal-build.mjs`. There are no downloaded character meshes or AI concept images in these production assets. The model has a matte armored head with a recessed two-eye visor and brow shutters, a compact torso, articulated arms and mitten hands, magnetic feet, and three differently sized gyroscopic rails. The outer rail has a deliberate opening and one progress marker.

Each GLB uses required `EXT_meshopt_compression`, `KHR_mesh_quantization`, and `KHR_texture_basisu` extensions. All geometry is merged into one skinned mesh with four material primitives. LOD files are alternatives: render one at a time. The shared atlas is embedded once in each self-contained GLB and is also exported separately. Baked analytic contact occlusion is stored in `COLOR_0`; material UVs reference padded atlas regions. There are no real-time shadows, particles, morph targets, physics, or post-processing requirements.

## Runtime contract

Coordinates are Y-up and +Z-facing, centered around the origin. A camera at `[3, 1, 8]`, targeting `[0, 0, 0]`, with an orthographic height of approximately 4.2 gives a restrained three-quarter view. Material names are `Gimbal_Ceramic`, `Gimbal_Metal`, `Gimbal_Face`, and `Gimbal_Accent`. Only `Gimbal_Accent` receives state color and emissive updates. The ceramic, brushed bearings, and dark face recess keep their neutral materials.

| Authoritative state | Clip | Duration | Playback | Static/reduced-motion pose |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `planning` | `planning` | 2.0 s | Loop; three stepped alignments with dwell intervals, gaze and arranging hand | `pose_planning` |
| `awaiting_approval` | `awaiting_approval` | 1/30 s | Static centered open-palms hold | `pose_awaiting_approval` |
| `applying` | `applying` | 1.2 s | Loop; clockwise outer rail, one attached marker, braced arms and feet | `pose_applying` |
| `verified` | `verified` | 0.7 s | Once; alignment, compact nod, single 3.5% ring expansion, then clamp | `pose_verified` |
| `blocked` | `blocked` | 0.25 s | Once; stop palm and locked off-axis rail settle, then clamp | `pose_blocked` |

All twenty directed transitions are included as `transition_<from>_to_<to>` and last 0.2 s. All clips are sampled at 30 fps or less and simplified. Every pose clip is entirely static; it can be sampled and rendered once. Transitions interpolate rotations between static poses. On an interrupted loop, the runtime may cross-fade from the current animation into the destination clip to preserve continuity. Update state text, icon, accent, and destination pose from the same typed state. Never derive state from assistant prose.

The skeleton has a root, torso, head, two eye pivots, shoulder/elbow/hand on each side, two feet, three ring pivots, and one outer-ring marker: 17 bones. Limbs are rigidly weighted, avoiding continuous deformation artifacts in mechanical joints. All clips explicitly reset ring scale so interruption of the verified pulse cannot leave an enlarged ring in another state.

The runtime layers short personality gestures over those workflow clips: an eye wink or a small hand wave every randomized 8–18 seconds (14–26 in low power). Applying and Blocked use a brief blink to retain their operational pose. Hover adds a glance; tap or keyboard activation greets. A three-second interaction cooldown prevents repeated restarts. Expressions restore the underlying pose before every animation sample and yield to workflow transitions. Still/reduced motion disables all expressions; hidden or offscreen characters cancel their timer, with a fresh interval on return. Settled states use no animation frames between gestures. The rings and authoritative state are never changed by a greeting.

## Regeneration and verification

Run `node scripts/gimbal-build.mjs` to regenerate both GLBs and every sheet/fallback. The build uses Three.js procedural geometry, glTF Transform, Meshoptimizer, `ktx2-encoder`, and Sharp. It fails if either LOD exceeds its triangle budget. Static sheets are rendered from those same meshes with a deterministic CPU depth buffer and smooth normal shading, not an illustrative substitute for the delivered model.

Every generated PNG includes a `tEXt` chunk with the key `prompt`, recording its original procedural origin and reproducible geometry source. `scripts/gimbal-png.mjs` adds this metadata without changing image samples. The regeneration script also copies the Basis README and the vendored license and attribution from `scripts/gimbal-licenses` next to the unchanged runtime decoder files.

Run `node scripts/gimbal-verify.mjs` to decode the delivered Meshopt assets and verify geometry counts, finite attributes, valid indices, material and skeleton budgets, one shared 1024² Basis atlas, all 30 named clips, transition durations, and motionless reduced-motion poses. Browser/GPU rendering still needs runtime verification; these measurements are asset budgets, not a hardware performance claim. The character name and design have not undergone trademark clearance or user recognition studies.
